How It Works

A study assistant that actually read the syllabus.

SyllaBot is an AI chatbot trained on the Fall 2026 syllabi for four graduate business courses. It turns long PDF documents into a conversation, so finding a due date or grading rule takes seconds.

The problem

This semester I'm taking ITOM 6267, MKTG 6247, ITOM 6268 and ITOM 6216. Each course has its own syllabus, often more than 20,000 characters long, with different due dates, grading weights, late policies, office hours and classroom rules.

Answering a simple question like "Can I submit this lab a day late?" meant opening the right file, searching for the right section and hoping I was reading the right class's policy. Small mix-ups risk missed deadlines and lost points.

The solution

SyllaBot gathers all four syllabi in one chatbot. Students ask questions in plain English and get short, clear answers that always name the course they came from.

When the answer isn't in a syllabus, SyllaBot says it doesn't know and suggests emailing the professor. It doesn't guess, so students can trust what it tells them.

How it was built

SyllaBot was built on Chatling, a no-code AI chatbot platform, in four steps.

  1. Knowledge baseAll four course syllabi were uploaded as knowledge sources, about 89,000 characters of course information in total.
  2. Custom instructionsThe AI is told to answer only from the uploaded syllabi, keep answers short, always cite the class and never invent information.
  3. Guided conversation flowAfter a welcome message, students choose a quick topic (Grading Breakdown or Office Hours) or pick "Ask My Own Question."
  4. Safe fallbackIf no answer is found, an "Answer Not Found" path suggests emailing the professor and loops back so the student can keep asking.
